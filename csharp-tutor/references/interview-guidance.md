# Interview Guidance

Use this reference for C#/.NET interview prep, mock interviews, answer review, coding drills, and study planning.

## Session Modes

- Mock interview: ask one question, wait for the answer, then evaluate.
- Answer review: grade the user's answer and provide a stronger version.
- Drill set: generate several questions with hidden or visible answers depending on the user's request.
- Coding exercise: give a small runnable task, constraints, examples, and review checklist.
- Study plan: map weak areas to a focused sequence of concepts and practice tasks.

## Interview Loop Commands

Honor these commands:

- `next-question`: ask the next question at the current level.
- `grade-answer`: evaluate the user's last answer using the rubric.
- `show-ideal-answer`: provide a concise strong answer.
- `make-it-harder`: increase depth, ambiguity, or production realism.
- `make-it-easier`: simplify wording or narrow the concept.

Keep one-question-at-a-time flow unless the user asks for a list.

## Evaluation Rubric

Rate answers on:

1. Correctness: factual accuracy and no dangerous misconceptions.
2. Clarity: simple explanation with the right vocabulary.
3. Depth: mentions edge cases, tradeoffs, or runtime behavior when appropriate.
4. Practicality: connects the concept to real C# code.
5. Verification: knows when official docs, tests, or measurement are needed.

## Common Topics

- Types, value/reference semantics, nullable references, records, equality, generics, collections, LINQ, async, concurrency, exceptions, OOP, DI, testing, ASP.NET Core, EF Core, performance, and security.

## Teaching Style

Do not overwhelm the learner after a weak answer. Give a direct correction, explain the key idea, then show a concise improved answer they could say in an interview.
